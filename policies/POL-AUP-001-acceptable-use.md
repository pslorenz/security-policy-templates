## 1. Purpose

This policy explains how [ORGANIZATION_NAME] expects employees, contractors, and other users to use company technology, data, and accounts. It covers what is acceptable, what is not, and what to do when something goes wrong.

The goal is straightforward. Every person using company resources should understand the basic rules. The rules are not designed to make work harder. They protect the company, the people we serve, and the user themselves from common problems that come from misusing technology.

This policy is read and acknowledged by every user before they receive access to [ORGANIZATION_NAME] systems. It is reviewed annually by leadership and updated as needed.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to:

All employees, contractors, temporary workers, interns, and volunteers of [ORGANIZATION_NAME]. Any vendor or partner with access to [ORGANIZATION_NAME] systems or data. Any other person granted access to [ORGANIZATION_NAME] resources for any reason.

It applies to:

All [ORGANIZATION_NAME] systems, networks, accounts, applications, and data, regardless of where they are hosted. All devices used to access [ORGANIZATION_NAME] resources, whether company-issued or personal. All work performed using [ORGANIZATION_NAME] resources, on or off company premises, during or outside business hours.

The policy applies the same way to a desktop in the office, a laptop at home, a phone in an airport, and a tablet in a coffee shop. The location does not change the rules.

## 3. Policy Statements

### 3.1 General Use

Company resources are for company work. Reasonable personal use (checking a personal email during a break, paying a bill at lunch, looking up directions for an after-work appointment) is acceptable. Substantial personal use that interferes with work, consumes significant company resources, or creates risk for the company is not.

When using company resources, users:

Follow this policy and the other policies referenced in Section 6. Use good judgment. If something feels questionable, ask before doing it. Treat company technology with reasonable care, like they would their own. Report problems, suspected security issues, and lost or damaged equipment promptly.

Users do not have a personal right to privacy in their use of company resources. [ORGANIZATION_NAME] may monitor, log, and review the use of its systems for legitimate business reasons, including security, performance, troubleshooting, legal compliance, and policy enforcement. Personal communications conducted on company systems are not private.

### 3.2 Account Security

Users protect their accounts and the credentials that access them.

Each user has their own account. Sharing accounts is not allowed. If two people need to do the same work, they each get their own account.

Users do not share their passwords with anyone, including IT staff, managers, or coworkers. IT will never ask for a password. Anyone who does is impersonating IT or doing something they should not be.

Users use the company-approved password manager (specified in POL-IAM-002) for all work credentials. Passwords are not stored in browsers on shared devices, in email, in chat, in spreadsheets, or in notes apps.

Users enable multi-factor authentication on their accounts and keep their authentication methods (hardware key, authenticator app, recovery codes) physically secure. Lost or stolen authentication devices are reported immediately so they can be disabled.

Users do not attempt to access systems or data they have not been authorized for. If a user discovers they have access to something they should not, they report it rather than exploring or using it.

### 3.3 Data Handling

Company data is handled according to its sensitivity. The data classification policy ([DATA_CLASSIFICATION_POLICY], forthcoming) defines the classification levels and the specific handling rules for each. In summary:

**Public data** (information that has been intentionally released for public consumption) can be shared freely.

**Confidential data** (most internal company information, employee information, client information, financial details) is shared only with authorized people, transmitted securely, and stored in approved systems.

**Restricted data** (the most sensitive information, including authentication credentials, regulated data, and the most sensitive client information) has additional handling requirements documented in the data classification policy.

For all classifications, users:

Store data in approved systems (Microsoft 365, Google Workspace, approved SaaS platforms, approved file shares). They do not store company data in personal accounts, personal cloud services, or unapproved tools. Do not email company data to personal addresses. Do not transfer data to personal devices or removable media without authorization. Do not post company data, client data, or sensitive information on social media, public forums, or other public venues.

When in doubt about whether something can be shared or with whom, users ask before sharing.

### 3.4 Email and Communications

Users represent [ORGANIZATION_NAME] in their work communications. The standard for email and messaging is professional courtesy.

Specifically:

Email and chat messages are written with the awareness that they may be retained, reviewed, forwarded, or produced in a legal proceeding. Communications that the user would not want a manager, a client, or a court to read are best not sent at all.

Phishing attempts, scam emails, and other suspicious messages are reported to the IT or Information Security function rather than acted on. The reporting process should be made simple ([REPORT_PHISHING_PROCESS]). Reporting a suspected phishing email is never a problem, even if it turns out to be legitimate.

Auto-forwarding of company email to personal accounts is not permitted. If a user needs access to email outside the company environment, they use the approved mobile and remote access tools.

Users do not send mass external communications (newsletters, announcements, marketing emails) without authorization through the appropriate channels. Marketing and external communications go through the marketing or communications function.

### 3.5 Internet and Web Browsing

Reasonable use of the internet for company work is expected. Reasonable personal use during breaks is acceptable. Specific things that are not acceptable:

Visiting websites that contain illegal content, sexually explicit content, content promoting violence or hate, or other material that creates legal or reputational risk for [ORGANIZATION_NAME]. Downloading software, browser extensions, or other code without authorization. Many seemingly harmless tools introduce serious security risks. Bypassing or attempting to bypass security controls (web filters, content filters, network restrictions). The controls exist for reasons. Streaming significant amounts of personal video or audio that affects network performance for others. Using company resources for any commercial activity outside [ORGANIZATION_NAME]'s business.

If a user has a legitimate business need to access something the security controls block, they request the access through the appropriate process.

### 3.6 Use of Generative AI Tools

Generative AI tools (ChatGPT, Claude, Microsoft Copilot, Google Gemini, GitHub Copilot, and similar) are powerful, useful, and easy to misuse. The basic rule: company-approved AI tools are encouraged for work where they help, and unapproved tools are not used with company data.

Approved AI tools at [ORGANIZATION_NAME] are [APPROVED_AI_TOOLS, e.g., Microsoft Copilot for M365, Claude Enterprise, ChatGPT Enterprise]. Approved tools have business agreements that protect company data, do not use company data to train public models, and offer appropriate logging and oversight.

When using approved AI tools, users:

Treat AI tool outputs with the same care as any other source. AI-generated content can be wrong, biased, or fabricated. Users review and verify what the tool produces before relying on it.

Do not paste confidential or restricted data into a public or consumer AI tool. The free public versions of AI services are not approved for company data, even if the company has an enterprise agreement for the same vendor.

Do not use AI tools to generate content that misrepresents the user's identity (deepfakes, impersonation, false statements presented as the user's own work in contexts where authenticity matters).

Do not use AI tools to circumvent other policies. An AI tool that helps the user produce something faster does not change whether the underlying activity is acceptable.

For code-generating AI tools (GitHub Copilot, Cursor, similar), the user remains responsible for the code that ends up in production. Generated code is reviewed for security, correctness, and license compliance the same as code written by hand.

When in doubt about whether an AI tool is approved or how it can be used, users ask the IT or Information Security function before using it.

### 3.7 Personal Devices

Users may access certain [ORGANIZATION_NAME] resources from personal devices (smartphones, tablets, personal laptops) under the following conditions.

The personal device meets the requirements in POL-IAM-005 (Remote Access Policy). Specifically, mobile devices are enrolled in the company device management system, and personal computers used for work are encrypted and meet basic security standards.

Company data on personal devices is accessed through approved applications (the official Microsoft 365 mobile apps, the official Google Workspace mobile apps, the approved remote access tool). Company data is not synced to personal cloud services, personal storage, or personal email.

The company has the right to remove its data from a personal device. If the user leaves the company, loses the device, or is found in violation of this policy, company data on the device may be wiped. The wipe targets only company data and does not affect personal data when the device is enrolled correctly.

Users who do not want to use their personal devices for work are not required to. The company will provide appropriate equipment.

### 3.8 Software and Tool Installation

Users do not install software, browser extensions, command-line tools, or other code on company-managed devices without authorization.

The approved software list is maintained by the IT function. Software not on the approved list is requested through the appropriate process. Most reasonable requests are approved within a few days; the process exists primarily to make sure the company is aware of what is running on its systems and can support it.

For personal devices used for work, the rules are lighter but still meaningful. Users do not install software that compromises the security of company data accessed on the device. Pirated software, untrusted browser extensions, and similar are not used on devices that touch company systems.

Open-source tools, freeware, and free SaaS services are software too. The same approval process applies.

### 3.9 Reporting Problems and Incidents

Users are the company's first line of detection for many security and operational issues. Prompt reporting is far more important than perfect understanding of what happened.

Users report:

Suspected phishing or social engineering attempts. Suspected account compromise (unexpected password reset emails, unrecognized sign-in alerts, suspicious activity on their accounts). Lost or stolen company devices. Lost or stolen authentication devices (hardware keys, phones with authenticator apps). Accidental disclosure of company data (a wrong recipient, a wrong attachment, a public post that should have been private). Suspicious behavior on company systems, whether by people or by automated activity. Anything else that feels wrong.

Reporting goes to the IT or Information Security function through [REPORTING_CHANNEL, e.g., a dedicated email address, a helpdesk ticket, a Slack channel].

Users who report problems in good faith are not penalized for the report, even if the problem turns out to be the user's mistake. Mistakes happen. Hidden mistakes become incidents.

### 3.10 Departure and Transition

When a user leaves [ORGANIZATION_NAME] or changes roles, certain obligations apply.

Departing users return company-issued equipment (laptops, phones, security keys, badges, documents) on or before their last day.

Departing users do not retain company data after departure. Personal copies of company files, customer lists, contact information, internal documentation, source code, or any other company-owned material are not kept, transferred, or used after the user's access ends.

Users who change roles internally have their access reviewed and updated to match the new role. Old access that is no longer needed is removed.

Confidentiality obligations and any specific contractual obligations (non-disclosure agreements, intellectual property assignments, non-compete clauses where applicable and enforceable) survive departure per the user's employment or contractor agreement.

## 4. Roles and Responsibilities

**Information Security function.** Owns this policy. Investigates suspected violations. Approves exceptions. Maintains the lists of approved tools, AI services, and software.

**IT function (or IT service provider).** Implements the technical controls that support this policy. Maintains the approved tool lists. Supports users in following the policy.

**Human Resources function.** Coordinates user onboarding, departure, and role changes. Ensures every user reads and acknowledges this policy. Handles disciplinary action arising from policy violations.

**Managers.** Set the example for their teams. Address minor violations directly. Escalate serious violations through HR and Information Security.

**All users.** Read this policy and ask questions about anything unclear. Follow the policy. Report problems and suspected violations promptly. Acknowledge the policy at onboarding and at each annual review.

## 5. Enforcement

Violations of this policy may result in:

Verbal or written warnings. Mandatory retraining. Loss of specific access privileges. For serious or repeated violations, termination of employment or contract. For violations that involve illegal activity, referral to law enforcement.

The severity of the response is proportionate to the violation. Honest mistakes reported promptly are handled differently than deliberate misconduct. The intent here is to maintain a workable security posture, not to punish minor lapses.

Specific situations:

Repeated phishing email failures (clicking on simulated phishing tests, falling for real phishing repeatedly): mandatory retraining, escalation to manager.

Deliberate violation of data handling rules (sending confidential data to personal accounts, posting client information publicly): formal disciplinary action consistent with HR policies.

Use of unauthorized AI tools with company data after warning: formal disciplinary action, possible loss of access to AI tools.

Theft of company data, intentional misuse of access, or other malicious activity: termination and potential legal action.
